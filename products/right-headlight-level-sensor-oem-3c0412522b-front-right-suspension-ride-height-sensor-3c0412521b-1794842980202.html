Right Headlight Level Sensor OEM 3C0412522B for A3 Q3 Front Right Suspension

What is OEM 3C0412522B?

This is a front-right suspension ride height sensor, also known as a headlight level sensor, for vehicles that use automatic headlight leveling. It mounts between the chassis and the lower suspension arm to measure ride height and sends that signal to the headlight control module so the beams stay aimed correctly as the car is loaded or drives over uneven ground.

Product Application & Use Cases

This OEM 3C0412522B is engineered for the following applications:

Headlight leveling system

  • Measures front-right ride height
  • Sends position signal to headlight control module
  • Keeps low beams aimed at the correct road level
  • Prevents glare to oncoming traffic when the car is loaded

Suspension height monitoring

  • Links the chassis to the lower suspension arm
  • Detects changes in ride height from passengers or cargo
  • Works with the vehicle's electronic systems that rely on ride height data

Signs your ride height sensor is failing

A bad front-right level sensor usually shows up as headlight aim problems or a warning light, because the system no longer knows the true ride height.

  • Headlight beams point too low or too high and do not adjust when the car is loaded
  • Headlight leveling warning light or message on the dashboard
  • Headlights flicker or move erratically while driving
  • The headlight aim changes after going over a bump or around a corner
  • Visible damage to the sensor linkage, arm, or connector
  • Corrosion or broken wires at the sensor plug

If the headlights are misaimed, have the sensor and linkage checked promptly; driving with incorrect aim can dazzle other drivers or reduce your own visibility.

Installing this OEM 3C0412522B is a straightforward process. The full job takes about 45-60 minutes.

1

Raise and secure the vehicle

Park on a level surface, chock the rear wheels, and lift the front right corner. Support it securely with jack stands or use ramps so you can reach the sensor behind the wheel.

2

Locate the sensor

Find the sensor mounted between the chassis and the lower suspension arm on the front right side. It has a small linkage arm and an electrical connector.

3

Disconnect the electrical plug

Release the locking tab on the connector and pull it straight off the sensor. Avoid pulling on the wires.

4

Remove the sensor

Unbolt the sensor from its chassis bracket and detach the linkage from the suspension arm. If the fasteners are rusted, apply penetrating oil and clean the threads before removing.

5

Install the new sensor

Mount the new sensor in the same position, reconnect the linkage to the suspension arm, and tighten the fasteners. Make sure the linkage moves freely through its range.

6

Reconnect and check

Plug the electrical connector back in until it clicks. Lower the vehicle and turn the ignition on. The headlight leveling system may need a short drive or a scan tool calibration to relearn the new sensor.

7

Verify operation

Check that the headlight warning light is off and that the beams move when you load the rear of the car or press down on the front right corner. If the light stays on, have the system scanned for codes.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

Do I need to reprogram or calibrate the system after replacing this sensor?
Many vehicles require a basic setting or calibration of the headlight leveling system after a sensor replacement. This is usually done with a scan tool. If you do not have one, a short drive may allow the system to relearn, but check your owner's manual or consult a mechanic if the warning light remains.
Can I replace just the front right sensor, or should I replace both sides?
It is acceptable to replace only the faulty sensor if the other side is working correctly. However, if both sensors are the same age and one has failed, the other may not be far behind. Inspect the opposite sensor for corrosion or damage before deciding.
Will a bad ride height sensor affect my headlight beam pattern?
Yes. The sensor tells the headlight control module the ride height, and the module adjusts the beam aim accordingly. A failed sensor can leave the beams too low, too high, or moving erratically, which affects visibility and can cause glare.
What should I check if the new sensor does not fix the warning light?
Check the wiring and connector for corrosion or damage, and make sure the linkage is properly attached and moves freely. Also verify that the sensor part number matches the original. If the light persists, have the system scanned for fault codes to rule out the control module or other sensors.
