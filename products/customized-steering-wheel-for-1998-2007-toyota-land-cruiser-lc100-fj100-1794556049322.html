Signs your steering wheel needs replacement

A worn or damaged steering wheel can affect comfort and control, so watch for these common issues.

  • Visible cracks or splits in the rim
  • Smooth, shiny surface that slips in your hands
  • Loose or wobbly feeling when turning
  • Peeling or flaking material
  • Excessive play in the wheel before the road wheels respond
  • Discoloration or fading that cannot be cleaned

If you notice excessive play or a loose wheel, stop driving and have the steering system inspected immediately.

Tools Required
Socket set, torque wrench, steering wheel puller, screwdriver, battery disconnect tool
1

Disconnect the battery

Disconnect the negative battery terminal and wait a few minutes to let any airbag system discharge.

2

Remove the airbag

If equipped, remove the airbag module by releasing the retaining clips or screws behind the wheel, then unplug the connector.

3

Loosen the center nut

Loosen the steering wheel retaining nut but do not remove it completely yet.

4

Pull the old wheel

Use a steering wheel puller to separate the wheel from the column splines, then remove the nut and the old wheel.

5

Install the new wheel

Align the new wheel with the column splines and slide it on, then install and torque the center nut to the factory specification.

6

Reconnect the airbag

Plug in the airbag connector and secure the airbag module back into place.

7

Reconnect the battery

Reconnect the negative battery terminal and check that the horn and any steering wheel controls work.
