Signs Your Water Pump Is Failing

A failing water pump often announces itself through coolant loss, noise, or overheating—catch it early to avoid engine damage.

  • Coolant leaking from the weep hole on the pump housing
  • Whining or grinding noise from the front of the engine that changes with RPM
  • Engine overheating or temperature gauge climbing above normal
  • Loose or wobbling fan/pulley caused by a worn bearing
  • Coolant residue or crust around the pump area
  • Heater output fluctuating or weak

If the temperature gauge spikes or you see steam, stop driving and let the engine cool—overheating can warp heads and ruin gaskets.

Installation Guide

Installing this OEM 12681417 is a straightforward process. The full job takes about 2-4 hours.

Tools Required
Socket set, wrench set, drain pan, coolant, gasket scraper, torque wrench (for reassembly), pulley removal tool if needed
1

Drain coolant

With the engine cold, place a drain pan under the radiator and open the drain petcock to empty the cooling system.

2

Remove accessory belt

Relieve tension on the serpentine belt and slip it off the water pump pulley.

3

Disconnect hoses and fan

Remove the fan shroud and clutch if necessary, then disconnect the radiator and heater hoses from the pump.

4

Unbolt old pump

Remove the mounting bolts and carefully pry the old pump away from the block. Clean the mating surface.

5

Install new pump

Apply a thin bead of gasket maker if required, then mount the new pump with a new gasket and torque bolts evenly.

6

Reassemble and refill

Reconnect hoses, reinstall the belt and fan, then fill with the correct coolant mix. Run the engine and check for leaks.

What coolant should I use after installing this pump?
Use the coolant type specified in your owner’s manual for your engine (often Dex-Cool for these GM applications). Mix 50/50 with distilled water unless using pre-mixed coolant.
How can I tell if my water pump is bad without removing it?
Look for coolant weeping from the pump weep hole, listen for bearing noise, and check for a loose pulley. A failing pump may also cause overheating or poor heater performance.
